AI chatbots and AI tutors for online courses, coaching and memberships: disclosure, sign-ups and the withdrawal right

Last reviewed 6 November 2026

Short answer. Say it is an AI in the first message, for a sales bot and for an AI tutor inside the course. A recorded course is digital content: the buyer keeps the 14-day withdrawal right unless they gave express consent to start, acknowledged losing the right, and got a confirmation on a durable medium (Article 16(m)). Live coaching is a service, so starting early needs an express request and a withdrawal costs a pro rata amount. An AI tutor that grades work in a way that steers a learner's progress may be high-risk under Annex III point 3, and inferring learners' emotions is prohibited in education institutions.

What applies

Part of the business Rule What to do
Sales chatbot, AI tutor, AI coach AI Act Article 50(1) and (5) Say it is an AI, at the latest at the first interaction
Recorded course or downloads Consumer Rights Directive Article 16(m) Consent, acknowledgement and a confirmation, or the 14 days stay
Live coaching, cohort calls Consumer Rights Directive Articles 8(8), 9 and 14(3) Express request to start early; pro rata charge on withdrawal
Membership renewals Consumer Rights Directive Article 6(1)(o); national law Price per period, renewal and how to cancel, before sign-up
AI grading and placement AI Act Annex III point 3 Keep a human on decisions that affect progress or certificates
Webcam or voice "engagement" tracking AI Act Article 5(1)(f) Don't infer learners' emotions

The AI disclosure, twice

Course businesses tend to use AI in two places: a sales chatbot on the landing page, and an AI tutor or coach inside the course. Both interact directly with people, so Article 50(1) applies to both, and the person must be told at the latest at the first interaction (Article 50(5)).

The tutor is the one that gets missed, especially when it speaks in the course creator's name or voice:

"I'm the course's AI tutor, trained on Maria's lessons. I can explain the material and point you to the right module. For feedback from Maria herself, use the weekly Q&A."

If the tutor uses a cloned voice or a video avatar of the creator, that is synthetic content of a real person, which brings Article 50(4) and the creator's consent into it (see the deep-fake guide). The disclosure wording guide has more first-message options.

Recorded courses: the withdrawal right is lost only one way

A recorded course, a template pack or a downloadable workbook sold online is digital content not supplied on a tangible medium. The buyer has 14 days to withdraw, unless all of these apply (Article 16(m), as amended by Directive (EU) 2019/2161):

  1. the consumer gave prior express consent to start the supply during the withdrawal period;
  2. the consumer acknowledged that they thereby lose the right of withdrawal; and
  3. the trader gave confirmation under Article 7(2) or 8(7), which for an online sale means a confirmation on a durable medium, such as an email.

A sales bot that says "you get instant access, no refunds on digital products" does not meet this. The consent and the acknowledgement belong at checkout, as two clear statements the buyer ticks, and the confirmation email repeats them. Without all three, the buyer can still withdraw within the 14 days. The bot can explain the policy, but it should point to the checkout step, not replace it.

Live coaching and cohorts: a service

One-to-one coaching, group calls and cohort programmes are services. The general rule applies: 14 days to withdraw from the conclusion of the contract (Article 9). If the client wants the first session inside those 14 days, they have to make an express request, which for an online sale the trader must have on a durable medium (Article 8(8)). If they then withdraw, they pay "an amount which is in proportion to what has been provided" (Article 14(3)). Without the express request, they pay nothing (Article 14(4)(a)).

Bundles mix both: a programme with recorded lessons and weekly calls has a digital-content part and a service part. Say at checkout which rule covers which part, and keep the bot's answer to the same text.

A live online class on a fixed date that is a leisure activity can fall under the Article 16(l) exception instead; the booking chatbot guide covers that case.

Memberships and renewals

For a subscription or membership, the buyer must get the price per billing period, the duration and the conditions for terminating before signing up (Article 6(1)(e) and (o)). A sales bot quoting "EUR 19 a month" should give the renewal terms in the same answer, or link to them. When members ask the bot how to cancel, the answer is the cancellation route and the notice period, not a retention script. National rules can add more: Germany requires a cancellation button for contracts concluded on a website, covered in the gym chatbot guide.

What an AI tutor should not decide

Grading and placement. Annex III point 3 of the AI Act lists as high-risk AI systems used to determine access or admission to education and vocational training, to evaluate learning outcomes (including where the result steers the learning process), to assess the level of education a person will receive or can access, and to monitor and detect prohibited behaviour during tests. Under the Digital Omnibus, the high-risk duties for Annex III systems apply from 2 December 2027.

Annex III point 3 is written for educational and vocational training institutions, and whether a small online course business counts as one is not settled. The practical line is clear enough either way. An AI tutor that explains, quizzes for practice and gives feedback is low risk. An AI tutor whose score decides whether a learner passes, gets a certificate or moves to the next level is the case the Annex describes. Keep a person on those decisions, and tell learners how to ask for a review.

Proctoring. Automated exam monitoring that flags cheating is listed in point 3(d). Avoid it unless you are ready for the high-risk duties.

Emotions. Article 5(1)(f) prohibits AI systems that infer the emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions, except for medical or safety reasons. It has applied since 2 February 2025. A feature that reads a learner's webcam or voice to measure "engagement" or "frustration" is the kind of system it targets. Don't build it into a course.

Learners' data. Course chats can contain personal stories, health details in coaching and work problems. Say in the privacy notice what the tutor stores, for how long, and whether the vendor trains on it.

Checklist

  • [ ] Sales bot and AI tutor both say they are AI in the first message.
  • [ ] A tutor speaking in the creator's voice or likeness is labelled, with the creator's consent.
  • [ ] Recorded courses: consent, acknowledgement and confirmation email at checkout.
  • [ ] Coaching: express request to start inside 14 days, on a durable medium.
  • [ ] Bundles say which part is digital content and which is a service.
  • [ ] Memberships show the price per period, renewal and cancellation terms before sign-up.
  • [ ] The bot answers cancellation questions with the route and notice period.
  • [ ] Pass/fail, certificates and level placement stay with a person.
  • [ ] No emotion inference from webcam or voice.
