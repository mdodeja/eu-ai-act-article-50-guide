AI chatbots for banks and lenders: disclosure, explanations, advice and the credit decision

Last reviewed 9 November 2026

Short answer. Say it is an AI in the first message. Any interest rate or cost figure the bot gives is credit advertising and needs the standard information as a representative example (Article 8 of Directive (EU) 2023/2225, which applies from 20 November 2026). Before a loan is concluded, the lender must give adequate explanations of the offer, its effects and the consequences of missed payments (Article 12); a chatbot can help, but the explanation has to match the actual offer. A bot that recommends a specific loan to a specific customer is providing advisory services, with best-interest duties (Article 16). Creditworthiness scoring is high-risk under the AI Act, and automated assessments carry a right to human intervention (Article 18(8)).

What applies

What the bot does Rule What to do
First message AI Act Article 50(1) and (5) Say it is an AI, at the latest at the first interaction
Quotes a rate, an APR or a monthly payment Directive 2023/2225 Article 8 Representative example, all standard items, the borrowing warning
Explains an offer before signing Directive 2023/2225 Article 12 Explanations tied to the actual offer, including late-payment consequences
"Which loan should I take?" Directive 2023/2225 Article 16 Either a proper advisory service, or no recommendation
Assesses whether the customer can borrow AI Act Annex III point 5(b); Directive 2023/2225 Article 18 Keep it out of the chat; human intervention on request

The AI disclosure

A bank or lender's assistant interacts directly with customers, so Article 50(1) of the AI Act applies, and customers must be told at the latest at the first interaction (Article 50(5)). Banking assistants often have a name and a friendly persona; the first message still has to be plain:

"Hi, I'm Riverbank's AI assistant. I can explain our loans and cards, help you check an application and answer account questions. For a person, type 'adviser'."

The disclosure wording guide has more options.

Rates and repayments are advertising

The new Consumer Credit Directive, Directive (EU) 2023/2225, applies from 20 November 2026 through national law. Its Article 8 requires standard information in any credit advertising that indicates an interest rate or any figure relating to the cost of credit: the borrowing rate, the total amount of credit, the APR, the duration, the total amount payable and the instalments, as a representative example, plus a clear warning that borrowing costs money. The car dealership guide sets out the list in full.

A chatbot that says "personal loans from 6.9%" or "about EUR 210 a month" is giving such a figure. Generate finance answers from the product's own representative example, and don't let the model calculate or round figures itself.

Adequate explanations before the loan

Article 12 requires creditors, and credit intermediaries where applicable, to give consumers adequate explanations of the proposed credit agreement and any ancillary services, free of charge and before the agreement is concluded, so the consumer can judge whether it suits their needs and financial situation. The explanations must cover:

  • the pre-contractual information (Articles 10, 11 and 38 of the Directive), such as the standard information form;
  • the essential characteristics of the products proposed;
  • the specific effects they may have on the consumer, including the consequences of default or late payment;
  • for bundled ancillary services, whether each part can be terminated separately and what that means.

A chatbot is a natural place to answer "what happens if I miss a payment?" or "can I cancel the insurance and keep the loan?". The risk is that a generative bot answers from general knowledge of loans instead of this customer's offer. Ground those answers in the actual offer and terms, and log them. Member States may adapt how the explanations are given in justified cases, so check the national rules.

When suggestions become advice

Article 16 requires creditors and intermediaries to tell consumers explicitly whether advisory services are or can be provided. Where advice is given, the provider must gather information about the consumer's financial situation, preferences and objectives, consider a sufficiently large number of products in its range, recommend suitable ones, act in the consumer's best interests, and give the consumer a record of the recommendation. Before giving advice, it must say whether the recommendation covers only its own range or a wide range of the market, and any fee.

A bot that compares products against criteria the customer chooses is not giving a personalised recommendation. A bot that says "based on what you told me, the Flexi Loan is the right one for you" probably is. Decide which service the bot provides, set it up that way, and test for drift (see the pre-launch test script).

Article 16(5) also requires a warning when a credit agreement may induce a specific risk for the consumer given their financial situation. A bot running an advisory flow has to be able to give that warning, which is one more reason to build it from fixed rules rather than free text.

Keep the credit decision out of the chat

  • AI Act. AI systems intended to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons or establish their credit score are high-risk (Annex III, point 5(b)), except those used to detect financial fraud. Under the Digital Omnibus, the high-risk duties for Annex III systems apply from 2 December 2027. The lender's scoring system will carry those duties; the chatbot should not run its own version.
  • Consumer Credit Directive. The creditor must carry out a thorough assessment of the consumer's creditworthiness before the agreement (Article 18(1)). Where the assessment uses automated processing of personal data, the consumer can request human intervention, including a clear explanation of the assessment, the right to give their point of view and the right to ask for a review (Article 18(8)).

So the bot can explain how applications are assessed and route a declined customer to the human review, but it should not tell customers whether they will be approved, and it should not collect income and debt details in a free-text chat outside the application.

Checklist

  • [ ] First message says it is an AI assistant.
  • [ ] Every rate or repayment figure comes with the representative example and the warning.
  • [ ] The model never calculates or rounds finance figures.
  • [ ] Explanations about late payment, bundles and termination come from the actual offer.
  • [ ] The bank has decided whether the bot gives advice; if it does, the Article 16 process applies.
  • [ ] No approval predictions; declined customers are routed to human review.
  • [ ] Income and debt details are collected in the application, not the chat.
  • [ ] Explanations and recommendations are logged.
