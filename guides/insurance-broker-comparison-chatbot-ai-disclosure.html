AI chatbots for insurance brokers and comparison sites: disclosure, demands and needs, and when the bot is giving advice

Last reviewed 7 November 2026

Short answer. Say it is an AI in the first message. A chatbot that asks questions, compares policies and lets the customer buy is insurance distribution under Article 2(1)(1) of the Insurance Distribution Directive, so the broker's duties apply to what it says. Before any contract is proposed, the customer's demands and needs must be established and the proposal must fit them (Article 20(1)). If the bot says which policy would suit the customer best, that is advice and needs a personalised recommendation explaining why. AI used for risk assessment and pricing of individuals for life and health insurance is high-risk under Annex III point 5(c).

What applies

Part of the chat Rule What to do
First message AI Act Article 50(1) and (5) Say it is an AI, at the latest at the first interaction
Bot compares and lets the customer buy IDD Article 2(1)(1) Treat the bot as part of your distribution, with your duties
Bot asks about the customer's situation IDD Article 20(1) Establish demands and needs before proposing a contract
"Which one should I take?" IDD Article 20(1) Either a personalised recommendation with reasons, or no recommendation
Non-life product details IDD Article 20(5) Provide the product information document on a durable medium
Life and health pricing by AI AI Act Annex III point 5(c) High-risk; keep it out of the chatbot

The AI disclosure

An insurance chatbot interacts directly with customers, so Article 50(1) of the AI Act applies, and customers must be told at the latest at the first interaction (Article 50(5)):

"Hi, I'm Harbour Insurance Brokers' AI assistant. I can explain cover types, compare quotes from our panel and help you start an application. You can ask for one of our advisers at any time."

The disclosure wording guide has more options.

The bot is part of your distribution

The Insurance Distribution Directive (Directive (EU) 2016/97, the IDD) defines insurance distribution broadly. Article 2(1)(1) includes "the provision of information concerning one or more insurance contracts in accordance with criteria selected by customers through a website or other media and the compilation of an insurance product ranking list, including price and product comparison", when the customer is able to directly or indirectly conclude an insurance contract using a website or other media.

A chatbot that asks what cover the customer wants, shows matching policies with prices and passes them to a quote or purchase page does exactly that. The duties that apply to the broker or comparison site apply to the bot's part of the journey too. The exclusions in Article 2(2) cover the mere provision of information about insurance products, and information given incidentally in another professional activity, but both depend on the provider not taking additional steps to help conclude a contract. A bot that hands the customer to a buy button is taking that step.

Demands and needs before any proposal

Article 20(1) requires the distributor to specify, on the basis of information obtained from the customer, "the demands and the needs of that customer", and to give the customer objective information about the product in a comprehensible form. "Any contract proposed shall be consistent with the customer's insurance demands and needs."

For a chatbot, that means:

  • The questions that establish demands and needs (what needs covering, for whom, existing cover, budget, excess preferences) are a fixed part of the flow, not left to the model to improvise.
  • The bot shows only products consistent with the answers. A generative bot that "upsells" add-ons the customer said they don't need is a problem under this test.
  • The answers and the products shown are logged, so the firm can show later why a product was proposed.
  • Product facts (what is covered, exclusions, limits, excess) come from the insurer's documents, not the model's general knowledge of what such policies usually include.

Advice or no advice

Under the same Article, if advice is given before the contract, the distributor must provide the customer with "a personalised recommendation explaining why a particular product would best meet the customer's demands and needs".

Customers will ask the bot "which one should I choose?". There are two clean options:

  1. No advice. The bot compares objectively against the customer's criteria and says plainly that it doesn't recommend a product, with a route to an adviser. The bot must then avoid phrases such as "this one is best for you".
  2. Advice. The bot gives a recommendation, and the firm treats it as advice: a personalised recommendation with the reasons, recorded and given to the customer, under the firm's advice process and supervision.

The risk is the middle ground, where a bot set up as non-advised drifts into "I'd go with Policy B" in a free-text answer. Test for it before launch (see the pre-launch test script) and keep the comparison output structured.

Product information documents

For non-life products, the product information must be given in the standardised insurance product information document, "on paper or on another durable medium" (Article 20(5)). A summary in the chat is not a substitute. The bot can link to the document and summarise it, and the document itself goes to the customer before the contract, for example as a PDF in the quote email.

AI pricing for life and health cover

Annex III point 5(c) of the AI Act lists as high-risk AI systems intended to be used for risk assessment and pricing in relation to natural persons in the case of life and health insurance. Under the Digital Omnibus, high-risk duties for Annex III systems apply from 2 December 2027. A broker's chatbot that collects health details and estimates the customer's premium or risk class for life or health cover is close to this. Keep pricing with the insurer's own underwriting systems, which will carry the high-risk duties, and keep the chatbot to explaining and collecting what the application form needs.

Health questions in life and health applications are special category data under Article 9 of the GDPR. Collect them in the insurer's application form, not in a free-text chat transcript.

Checklist

  • [ ] First message says it is an AI assistant.
  • [ ] The comparison and purchase flow is treated as insurance distribution, with the firm's IDD duties.
  • [ ] Demands-and-needs questions are fixed and asked before any product is shown.
  • [ ] Only products consistent with the answers are shown; no unrequested add-ons.
  • [ ] Product facts come from the insurer's documents.
  • [ ] The firm has decided: advice (with a personalised recommendation) or no advice (and the bot never recommends).
  • [ ] Non-life product information documents go to the customer on a durable medium.
  • [ ] No AI risk scoring or pricing for life or health cover in the chatbot; health details go in the application form.
  • [ ] Chats are logged so the firm can show why a product was proposed.
